We do not have all the answers yet. AI's disruption will hit different communities, careers, and generations in ways we have not seen before, and will stretch our current systems beyond their capacity. The only way to find out what will work is to get into the field and test it.

We are already collaborating with networks of state leaders to identify pilot opportunities. Each pilot has pre-specified success criteria, independent evaluation, and a hard decision point: scale, revise, or kill. We will publish results, including failures.

Some of our pilot ideas include:

  • Young Workers: We are collaborating with two states to reinvent national service, modeled on the legal aid clinic concept, to provide entry-level experience. Other pilots: testing medical residency-type models for white-collar industries, entrepreneurship stipends for young people without a financial cushion, and employer experiments to rebuild how junior talent gets developed.
  • Displacement Response: We are exploring pilots to test wage insurance, expanded unemployment insurance for displaced knowledge workers, and retraining programs to understand what models (if any) actually work.
  • Worker Ownership and Agency: We will pilot sectoral bargaining frameworks for AI deployment decisions, test gain-sharing agreements at the firm level, and explore new models of worker ownership in AI-augmented industries.
  • Good Life Pilots: What does a good life look like when work no longer structures it? We will run real-world experiments testing new social institutions and employment models: reimagined public spaces, subsidized civic and creative roles, and community-led pilots where people themselves design what comes next.
